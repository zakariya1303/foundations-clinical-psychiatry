Overdose

A practical “before, during, after” framework for overdose assessment, plus high-yield initial management steps.

Overview

Overdose may involve intentional self-poisoning, accidental toxicity, or recreational substance misuse. Presentations vary significantly depending on the substance involved and may rapidly become life-threatening.

There is an important nuance to overdose history taking. A structured “before, during, after” framework can help ensure important psychosocial and medical details are not missed.

Patients presenting following overdose may feel frightened, ashamed, emotionally overwhelmed, or ambivalent about surviving, meaning assessment should remain calm, compassionate, and non-judgemental.

Key Symptoms

  • Reduced consciousness
  • Respiratory depression
  • Agitation
  • Confusion
  • Seizures
  • Vomiting
  • Arrhythmias
  • Pinpoint or dilated pupils
  • Suicidal ideation

Important Questions

Before: explore the emotional and situational context leading up to the overdose.

“What was happening before you took the overdose?”
“How were you feeling emotionally at the time?”
“Had anything stressful or upsetting happened recently?”
“Had you been thinking about harming yourself beforehand?”
“Had you made any preparations beforehand (for example leaving a note or message)?”
“Were you expecting someone to find you, or were you trying not to be found?”

During: clarify the specifics of the overdose itself.

Important areas include:

  • Substance taken
  • Quantity
  • Timing
  • Co-ingestion
  • Alcohol involvement
  • Witnesses
“What exactly did you take?”
“How much did you take?”
“Did you take anything else alongside it?”
“Did you think the overdose would be fatal?”

After: explore actions taken afterwards and ongoing intent.

“Did you tell anyone afterwards?”
“Did you seek help yourself?”
“How do you feel about the overdose now?”
“Are you still having thoughts of harming yourself?”

You should also assess:

  • Current suicide risk
  • Regret or ambivalence
  • Protective factors
  • Access to further means
  • Psychiatric history
  • Substance misuse
  • Social supports

Initial Management

  • A–E assessment
  • Assess airway and respiratory status
  • Monitoring and IV access
  • Blood glucose
  • ECG
  • Toxicology advice where needed
  • Activated charcoal in selected cases
  • Specific antidotes where appropriate
  • Psychiatric assessment once medically stable

Examples include:

  • Naloxone for opioid overdose
  • N-acetylcysteine for paracetamol overdose

Clinical Pearls

  • Always ask about co-ingestion
  • Time of ingestion significantly affects management
  • Patients with paracetamol overdose may initially appear well
  • Mental health assessment is essential following intentional overdose
  • Compassionate communication is extremely important
  • Structured “before, during, after” histories help avoid missing key information
