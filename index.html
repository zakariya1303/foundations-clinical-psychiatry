20 marks

Short Based Answer Questions

Answer 20 single best answer questions (SBAs). Submit to receive a score out of 20.

1. A 22-year-old woman presents with sudden lower limb weakness following an emotionally distressing argument. Neurological examination reveals inconsistent findings, including normal reflexes and fluctuating weakness that improves with distraction. MRI spine is normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
2. A 35-year-old man with a history of psychosis is admitted under the Mental Health Act. He is agitated, pacing, and becoming increasingly aggressive towards staff. What is the MOST appropriate initial pharmacological management?
3. A 31-year-old nulliparous woman presents for the first time in her pregnancy at 29 weeks. Her current medications include fluoxetine and lactulose. She wants to know what risk her medications might have to her baby. What is a potential risk of fluoxetine when used in the third trimester of pregnancy?
4. An 83-year-old woman with Alzheimer’s dementia becomes increasingly agitated in the evenings. She occasionally shouts at staff and tries to leave her care home. She has no hallucinations. Physical exam and bloods are unremarkable. What is the most appropriate management?
5. A 32-year-old woman is brought to her GP by a family member due to concerns about unusual beliefs. She is firmly convinced that a well-known television presenter is secretly in love with her and is attempting to communicate through subtle signs in interviews and social media posts, despite never having met them. She is otherwise calm and well kempt. There is no evidence of hallucinations, thought disorder, cognitive impairment, or mood disturbance. Mental state examination is otherwise unremarkable. What is the most appropriate name for this condition?
6. A 27-year-old man is reviewed on a psychiatric ward due to concerns regarding his thought processes. During the interview, he answers questions in an overly detailed and indirect manner. When asked how he has been feeling recently, he begins discussing a delayed train journey, then talks about a conversation he had with a colleague, before eventually returning to the original topic several minutes later. His speech is slow but coherent, and he eventually answers most questions after providing excessive unnecessary detail. What is the best description of this speech pattern?
7. A 31-year-old man with schizophrenia, stable on clozapine for 2 years, presents with 3 days of fever, sore throat, and lethargy. He has no cough. His clozapine level last month was normal. What is the most appropriate next step?
8. A 30-year-old man reports repeated intrusive thoughts that he will stab his partner in her sleep. He feels terrified by these thoughts and repeatedly checks knives are locked away. He says, “I know it’s irrational, but I can’t stop thinking it.” What is the most likely diagnosis?
9. A 22-year-old woman is brought after an overdose. On arrival she is drowsy but arousable. ECG shows QRS widening. There have been a few brief generalised myoclonic jerks. Her oxygen saturation is 98% on air. What is the most appropriate immediate step?
10. A 43-year-old woman with panic disorder on phenelzine presents with a sudden severe headache, palpitations, and sweating after a restaurant meal. BP is 198/112 mmHg. ECG and bloods are pending. What is the most likely diagnosis?
11. A 48-year-old man is admitted to the burns unit following injuries sustained in a house fire. Three days into his admission, nursing staff notice he has become increasingly restless and confused. Overnight, he becomes severely agitated and repeatedly attempts to remove his cannula, claiming there are insects crawling across the walls and inside his bedsheets. Security staff are required to help calm and restrain him. His partner later mentions that he drinks large amounts of alcohol daily. What is the most likely diagnosis?
12. A 30-year-old man is brought to the Emergency Department by police after being found wandering outside a train station appearing frightened and confused. He believes strangers are monitoring him through hidden cameras and says the television has been sending him coded messages specifically about his life. During assessment he appears guarded and internally preoccupied. He occasionally pauses mid-sentence for several seconds before continuing with an unrelated topic. He reports hearing two voices discussing his actions in the third person. Symptoms began gradually over the past 8 months. He has become socially withdrawn, lost his job, and stopped contacting friends. There is no clear history of elevated mood or major depressive episodes. What is the most likely diagnosis?
13. A 38-year-old man with schizophrenia is brought to hospital because he has barely moved or spoken for two days. On examination he maintains unusual postures for prolonged periods and resists passive limb movement. He occasionally repeats the examiner’s words. What is the most likely diagnosis?
14. A 44-year-old man with bipolar disorder attends his GP with his partner. Over the last several days, he has become increasingly unsteady and drowsy. His partner reports that his speech has become slurred and that he has developed a noticeable tremor. She also mentions that one of his long-term psychiatric medications was recently increased following a review. On examination, he appears confused with coarse tremor and impaired coordination. What is the most appropriate next step in management?
15. A 28-year-old man is started on haloperidol for acute psychosis. Two days later, he develops neck stiffness and his eyes are deviated upwards. What is the MOST likely diagnosis?
16. A 23-year-old university student attends her GP after encouragement from friends who have noticed longstanding fluctuations in her mood. Over the past 3 years, she has experienced repeated periods where she feels unusually energetic, productive, and sociable, often sleeping less than usual and taking on multiple new projects. These episodes are followed by weeks of feeling low in mood, unmotivated, and emotionally withdrawn. However, she has never experienced psychosis, required hospital admission, or had symptoms severe enough to meet the full diagnostic criteria for either mania or major depressive disorder. She continues to function academically, although her friends describe her as emotionally “up and down.” What is the most likely diagnosis?
17. A 56-year-old man with chronic alcohol dependence presents with unsteady gait and horizontal nystagmus. He is mildly confused. BP and pulse are normal. No hallucinations or tremor. What is the most appropriate management?
18. A 36-year-old man is brought to the Emergency Department with agitation, confusion, sweating, and fever several hours after taking an increased dose of his antidepressant medication alongside tramadol for back pain. On examination, he is tachycardic and hypertensive. He has marked hyperreflexia and inducible clonus in both lower limbs. Bowel sounds are hyperactive. What is the most likely diagnosis?
19. A 29-year-old woman presents to her GP with a long history of anxiety. She describes feeling constantly “on edge” and worries excessively about multiple areas of life including work, finances, health, and relationships. She struggles to control the worrying and reports poor sleep, muscle tension, and feeling restless most days for the past year. She still attends work and social events, although she finds them stressful. What is the most likely diagnosis?
20. A 41-year-old woman is brought to the Emergency Department by her husband due to sudden behavioural change over the past 12 hours. She is agitated, emotionally labile, and repeatedly insists that nurses are trying to poison her. During the assessment she becomes distracted, struggles to maintain attention, and incorrectly states the year and location. Her husband reports she was completely well yesterday but has recently had a productive cough and fever. What is the most likely diagnosis?
